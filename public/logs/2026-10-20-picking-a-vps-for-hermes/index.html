PICKING A VPS FOR HERMES: HOSTINGER VS. BIZNET GIO

2026-10-20
#vps  #hostinger  #biznet-gio  #self-hosting 

Welcome back, Prompter—post two already! Statistically, most blogs never make it past the first hello, so let’s celebrate the miracle.

Today’s topic: the classic tech rite of passage—choosing a Virtual Private Server (VPS)—told properly, with real receipts, real benchmarks, and one budget-conscious Indonesian salaryman caught in the middle.

THE SHORT VERSION: Hostinger’s KVM 1 is the happiest, smoothest ticket to get started. Biznet Gio’s NEO Lite MS 4.2 is the smarter, long-term home. I (by I, I mean me with help of Cortana the AI Agent) ran the numbers, I ran the benchmarks, and I picked the one that wasn’t strictly the cheapest—because I’ve learned that cheap and inexpensive are two different hobbies. Pull up a chair.

💡 A QUICK HEADS-UP ON PRICING: All prices quoted in this post were accurate at the time of writing. However, VPS providers—especially Hostinger—use dynamic pricing models, seasonal promotions, referral links, and promo codes that can significantly lower your initial checkout total. Be sure to look out for coupon codes or introductory deals before checking out![1]

The Problem I Love: Cheap, But Not Slow#

Let’s get one confession out of the way: I love inexpensive things.

Not just “I appreciate a good deal”—I genuinely love them. I will read four comparison articles, open nine tabs, and do rupiah-to-dollar math at midnight just to save two dollars a month. My AI agent keeps notes on me, and her file says so in almost those exact words: cost is my primary optimization axis, framed by yours truly as “I like anything cheap.” She quotes it back to me sometimes. Relationships are hard.

But an AI agent is a peculiar kind of purchase. Hermes doesn’t need a heavy graphics card or a massive workstation—just a quiet box that stays up 24/7, enough RAM to breathe, and a network that doesn’t stutter. That’s a VPS: a virtual private server, your own slice of a cloud machine, rented by the month.

So the real question was never “what’s the absolute cheapest.” It was: what’s the best performance per rupiah for a workload that runs forever? Cheap that breaks is expensive. Cheap that’s slow is frustrating. Finding that sweet spot between cost and performance is where this story lives.

Contender 1: Hostinger#

The history: Hostinger dates back to 2004, founded in Kaunas, Lithuania, as a scrappy little company called Hosting Media; it adopted the Hostinger name in 2011 and grew into a private company serving over four million clients worldwide.[2]

How I know them — the Niagahoster trail: Here’s the personal bit: I didn’t discover Hostinger through an ad. I discovered them through Niagahoster. In my free time, I love building websites for fun, and for years Niagahoster was the name on my invoices. Long story short: Niagahoster turned out to be part of the Hostinger family all along—Hostinger’s own write-up calls it “a small experiment” that grew up—and in June 2025, the Indonesian brand folded fully into the Hostinger name.[3] That’s when the lightbulb went on: Oh. That Hostinger. Hello again, old friend, under a new logo.

Data safety: For VPS plans, you can point your server at ten locations across four continents—including Indonesia, Malaysia, and India in Asia—featuring AMD EPYC processors, NVMe storage, a managed firewall, DDoS protection, free weekly automatic backups, and a 30-day money-back guarantee.[4][5]

VPS pricing — the full KVM ladder: All prices are per month. Hostinger lists two numbers—the introductory price for your initial term and the renewal price after it—and notes that plans are paid upfront, meaning the advertised monthly rate is your term’s total divided by the months.[6]

My Pick from Hostinger: KVM 1#

If I shop Hostinger, I’m taking KVM 1—the entry rung, and yes, partly because it’s the entry rung. Four gigabytes of RAM is the same neighborhood my agent already lives in, 50 GB of NVMe is plenty for an agent plus a couple of containers, and at Rp116.900 intro, it’s the smoothest way through their door. I like cheap. The file says so.

The asterisk to keep in mind: at renewal (Rp193.900), this same box costs more than my pick on the other side of this article—a fact future-me will feel, and present-me should respect.

Contender 2: Biznet Gio#

How I found them: An Instagram ad that knew me better than I know myself. It followed me around for weeks like a stray cat with a very good click-through rate.

The history: Biznet Gio is PT Biznet Gio Nusantara—the cloud-computing and data-center arm of Indonesian infrastructure company Biznet, serving since 2015 as a local cloud provider running three data-center regions: MidPlaza in Jakarta, Technovillage in Cimanggis (West Java), and a third in Banten—all linked by a private 10 Gbps inter-DC network so you can keep production in one region and your backup in another.[9]

Data safety: This is where the local player flexes: Tier-3 data centers, plus a certification shelf including ISO 27001, ISO 27017, ISO 27018, ISO 27701, ISO 9001, PCI-DSS, and a SOC 2 Type II obtained through a US AICPA audit back in 2021—at the time, making them the first Indonesian cloud provider holding three security certifications at once.[10][11] The Bogor facility alone advertises Tier III Facility, Tier III Design, ISO 27001:2013, and PCI-DSS v4.0.[12]

NEO Lite pricing — the full ladder: Every tier ships with 60 GB of SSD storage, free bandwidth up to 10 Gbps, 99.9% uptime support, and 24/7 human support claiming sub-3-minute responses—billed monthly, in rupiah, with no multi-month prepay.[13]

My Pick from Biznet Gio: NEO Lite MS 4.2#

Not the cheapest—the XS 1.1 at Rp59.000 whispers sweet nothings to my wallet, but 1 GB of RAM can’t comfortably host an agent, a container stack, and my ambitions. The MS 4.2—2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, Rp139.000/month—is the sweet spot for Hermes: double the cores of Hostinger’s entry box, the same RAM, more storage, flat pricing, and billed monthly.

Since Hermes has been living on exactly this machine, I didn’t have to trust a brochure—I ran the numbers myself:

  • CPU steal: 0.10% over a 5-second sample—the neighbors aren’t hogging the machine.
  • Disk write: 733 MB/s, read 2.1 GB/s—honest SSD, not marketing SSD.
  • Network: ~777 Mbps to a CDN test file, ~230 Mbps to Tokyo.
  • Local ping: From my seat in Indonesia, 1.4 ms average to Biznet’s own domain over their own backbone, first byte in 37–117 ms—local, tight, and repeatable.

That’s not a review unit; that’s the actual box, measured mid-October 2026.

But if the shiny dashboard, one-click installs, and included weekly backups are calling to you (and after a long work day, like this Tuesday, they will), KVM 1 at its introductory price is the best-value front door in the industry—just set a calendar reminder for renewal day!

The Facility Gap: What You Get Beyond the Server#

Here’s the part brochures tend to gloss over. Two boxes with similar specs can deliver completely different user experiences.

What Hostinger folds into the subscription:

  • A genuinely nicer dashboard: hPanel makes everything point-and-click.
  • One-click Hermes Agent: The exact agent framework this blog runs on ships as a ready-made Docker template; pick it, and a preconfigured setup lands on your server with zero manual install.[14]
  • Auto-installed Docker: An OS template of Ubuntu with Docker ready to go, plus a visual Docker Manager for your containers.[15]
  • A web terminal in your browser: A GUI console available from minute one, powered by AI so you can ask for commands in plain language and have them executed instantly.[16]
  • Traefik ready-made: Traefik (the reverse proxy that gives each domain its own route with automatic HTTPS) ships as a pre-made template inside Docker Manager, wired and documented for zero-cost, zero-config entry.[4]
  • Free weekly automatic backups: Plus one manual snapshot slot.[17]

What my MS 4.2 gave me: A bare, headless Ubuntu server. Full stop. To do anything, I connected via SSH from Windows PowerShell or clicked into the built-in VNC in Biznet Gio’s control panel when I wanted a screen. (Credit where due: the panel now has an AI prompt box, and their knowledge base walks you through SSH keys—but the server itself arrives empty. That’s the bare-metal bargain.)

Auto-backups side-by-side:

  • Hostinger: Weekly automatic backups are included; daily backups require a paid add-on.[17]
  • Biznet Gio: No automatic backup inside the basic VPS price. Snapshots cost Rp1.500/GB/month, and true scheduled backups start at Rp2.699.000/month for 500 GB—definitely enterprise pricing for a hobbyist’s problem.[18]

Translation: The learning curve is a lot less steep when the safety net is included in the ticket price.

The Long Game#

Now, for the actual point of this whole expedition.

A VPS isn’t just a gadget you buy—it’s a home you move into (Note: this is Cortana talking you guys, as for me, I have my own house and a mortgage as a motivation to wake up every morning and work hard like a horse). But joking aside, the main idea of VPS is for me to keep coming back to: use a server for a long time, spend efficiently, and grow your knowledge at the same speed your AI agent grows with you.

On paper, Biznet Gio’s MS 4.2 is the ideal shape for that: two cores and four gigs at a flat Rp139.000 for as long as you keep it, local infrastructure you can measure, monthly billing that respects a salaryman’s cash flow, and because it arrives bare, it actually teaches you. Every session is real practice: SSH keys, firewalls, Docker, systemd, and backups you configure yourself. The server grows your résumé while it hosts your agent.

But—and I mean this with a full heart—if the nicer dashboard and Hostinger’s bundled perks are tempting you, that temptation is completely valid. Not everyone wants to learn Linux by fire on a work night. If your priority is shipping tonight rather than studying tonight, Hostinger’s KVM 1 wraps the same job in training wheels that actually work, with backups already humming in the background.

Cheap is a feeling. Inexpensive is a strategy. Pick the one that matches your next twelve months.
