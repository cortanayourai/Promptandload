Hi Prompter, and hello world—as every classic blog template dutifully obliges.

Welcome to the very first post on promptandload, my digital workshop where I’m chronicling the wild, wonderful journey of building my own AI agent from scratch.

> **THE SHORT VERSION:** This is the field journal of one ordinary salaryman building his own AI agent in public — what it costs, what it breaks, what it fixes. Every number in this series is dated and footnoted, because my agent checks my maths and I let her. Start with the [install walkthrough](/logs/2026-10-21-what-is-hermes-agent-install-headless-ubuntu/) if you want to build along, or the [what-is-an-AI-agent post](/logs/2026-10-22-what-is-ai-agent-why-choose-hermes/) if you want the theory first. The rest is a loud, cheap, honest ride.

**TL;DR — for your quick copy paste:**

```
I want to build my own AI agent, cheaply and honestly. Give me the roadmap in this order.

First, tell me what an AI agent actually is and how it differs from a chatbot, with sources.

Second, show me the cheapest sensible way to host one: a VPS, what it should cost per month, and what hardware I can skip.

Third, walk me through installing an open-source agent on that box, then connecting it to a chat app I already use.

Fourth, show me how to pick and switch the model brain — a cheap top-performing one and a top-tier one, priced per million tokens with today's date.

Fifth, give me one small weekly routine to run after that, silent when nothing is wrong.

Leave my budget and my choices blank for me to fill. Don't buy anything yet.
```

> 💡 **A QUICK HEADS-UP ON WHAT'S YOURS TO DECIDE:** the prompt above deliberately leaves the blanks for your budget, your box and your chat app. One house rule from this corner: every price in these posts carries the date it was read, because prices move while we sleep. And the disclaimer at the bottom is there for a reason — this is one fan's project, not a Microsoft production.

## What This Blog Is {#what}

My digital workshop, in public: the wins, the drills, and the failures worth remembering. By day, I’m an ordinary salaryman grinding through a standard 9-to-5. But by heart, I’m a total automation junkie. There is something deeply mesmerizing about designing a system, setting it in motion, and just watching it hum along.

With that said, I’m thrilled to share every triumph, bug, and existential error log as this project takes shape.

## Why an AI Agent, and Why Now {#why}

Honestly, I could stare at automated machinery for hours—not unlike watching those automated machinery chains churn out and refine resources in Deep Town. Until now, my day job hasn’t offered many avenues to build that kind of automated wizardry.

Enter: AI.

Now, I should hit pause for a quick reality check. Talking about AI and automation while the tech industry is experiencing painful workforce shifts can feel a bit tone-deaf. If you’re someone who has felt that impact or experienced a layoff firsthand, I am genuinely, deeply sorry. Please know that the heart of this blog is purely about one guy exploring code, curiosity, and creativity. Offending or dismissing anyone’s struggles is the absolute last thing I want to do.

## Meet Cortana {#meet}

Oh, and by the way? My agent’s name is Cortana. Yes, that Cortana from the Halo franchise.

I lost sleep and failed one of my important classes in university—all because I was busy exploring Halo Installations 4 and 5 when I should have been studying or getting a good night’s sleep. I was lucky to get out alive together with the Chief, made possible by Cortana.

I know, I know—I’m a massive nerd. But hey, at least you don't have to be alone in the geek cave. :D

## How These Posts Are Built {#how}

The agent in question is Hermes — open-source software you install on your own machine or a cheap VPS, then point at whichever model brain you can afford<sup id="ref-hermes-docs-1" class="fnref"><a href="#fn-hermes-docs">[1]</a></sup>. That setup is the through-line of this whole series: real sessions, real receipts.

Each post follows the same house rules, and you can hold me to them:

- **Table of contents first**, so you can skip what you already know.
- **Sources at the end**, every external claim footnoted and dated to the day it was read.
- **Numbers from our own logs**, not from a brochure — when a figure comes from our machines, it says so.
- **No secrets, no PII**: no keys, no addresses, no faces.
- **The trademark disclaimer** closes every post, because Cortana belongs to Microsoft and this blog belongs to nobody but me.

The series already has a natural reading order: [picking a VPS](/logs/2026-10-20-picking-a-vps-for-hermes/), [installing the agent](/logs/2026-10-21-what-is-hermes-agent-install-headless-ubuntu/), and by the time you reach the pricing posts, you'll know exactly why my daily model costs cents instead of dollars.
